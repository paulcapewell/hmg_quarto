Case Study: Zellweger syndrome

A family is referred to clinical genetics with a severely ill new-born child who has received a clinical diagnosis of probable Zellweger syndrome. This is an autosomal recessive systemic disorder characterised clinically by severe neurologic dysfunction, craniofacial abnormalities, and liver dysfunction, and biochemically by the absence of peroxisomes. Zellweger syndrome is characterised by locus heterogeneity and can be caused by mutations in any one of several genes, encoding proteins known as pexins, which are involved in peroxisome biogenesis. In order to try and provide a genetic diagnosis, whole exome sequencing was performed in the family and variants in a known Zellweger gene, PEX3, detected:

The data strongly support a positive diagnosis of Zellweger syndrome in the affected child who is homozygous for two predicted high impact variants (one frameshift, one premature stop), that have not been previously observed in gnomAD. Although the impact of the 143784092 variant is predicted as moderate, this allele is relatively frequent (~2% and 17% in Latino’s), and at least 600 homozygotes have been observed, so unlikely to be a disease allele. The 143800237 variant is novel, but synonymous, so again unlikely pathogenic. Thus, both parents are heterozygous for disease alleles and future risk is ¼ children.
